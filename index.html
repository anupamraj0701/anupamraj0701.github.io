About

My current research focuses on the impact of AI on learning, teaching, and the labor market. I use quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods to understand how AI is reshaping classrooms and career pathways.

My work is guided by fundamental questions: What does it mean to learn? How can we learn effectively and humanly? And what does it mean to teach in a way that supports learning that is both effective and human? I hope to develop conceptual frameworks that are empirically rich and theoretically rigorous, with the goal of supporting historically marginalized communities and advancing equity in education.

Ongoing · Spencer Foundation

Black Youth and AI: Exploring the Experience and Perception of Black and Latino Youth in NYC with AI

Edmund W. Gordon Institute for Advanced Study, Teachers College, Columbia University

A mixed methods study of how Black and Latino high school youth in New York City understand, trust, and use AI. Early findings show that AI trust and AI use are meaningfully linked, while literacy and bias experience operate more independently.

Manuscript in Preparation

The Student's Dilemma: Using Game Theory and Mechanism Design to Realign AI Use in Education

With Andrew Gelman (Columbia) and Joseph McIntyre (Harvard)

This project models AI use in the classroom as a Prisoner's Dilemma style game between students. It explores how incentive structures could be redesigned so that using AI responsibly is also the individually rational choice.

Ongoing · AEQUITAS Lab

Epistemic Injustice in Teacher Perceptions of AI Research

With Renzhe Yu

A comparative review of research on teacher perceptions of AI finds that studies from non-WEIRD countries rely far more heavily on quantitative methods, with almost no room for qualitative or mixed methods approaches. This raises questions about whose perspectives get captured.
